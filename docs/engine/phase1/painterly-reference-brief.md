# Grounded painterly 3D: Barton Creek reference brief

This brief interprets the five still images, short tree video, and [Alexander Maznev's 3D landscape breakdown](https://80.lv/articles/breakdown-making-3d-landscape-look-like-painting) supplied by the founder in October 2026. The references describe a **direction**, not assets to reproduce. The target remains a geographically grounded, human-scale Barton Creek world that players can walk through and edit. Keep the source map and semantic objects separate from the rendering treatment. The [walking-height art fixture](art-reference.md) and [editable workshop captures](workshop-art-integration.md) are the current comparison baseline, not accepted final art.

The [80.lv interview](https://80.lv/articles/breakdown-making-3d-landscape-look-like-painting) describes an artist's **Unreal Engine** scene, not an EnFractal engine prescription. Useful transferable choices include treating trees as compositional anchors; broad color gradients and limited bark detail instead of photographic noise; clustering leaves into irregular brushlike masses; using terrain-aware grass colors, carefully placed branches, and vertex-color occlusion; and beginning with one directional light and restrained atmosphere. Blender, SpeedTree, Substance 3D, Houdini, Adobe Fresco, and Unreal are tools in that artist's workflow. We can author original models in Blender and render them in the existing Godot project; no tool switch is implied.

These are third-party reference images and a third-party article. Keep the supplied stills and video in the task context; do not commit their pixels, trace their geometry, use them as textures, or imply that EnFractal has a license to redistribute them. Record newly created assets and their rights separately.

## Barton Creek translation

The source terrain, creek/trail centerlines, roads, and building footprints stay pinned to their evidence. The art pass interprets their surfaces, edges, and near-field details. Unsurveyed individual plants, rock faces, creek width, and fabricated structures remain explicitly labeled authored interpretations. Existing modern mall and interchange forms remain contemporary: concrete, limestone-colored cladding, glass, steel, asphalt, and signage where evidence supports it. A player-created fantasy grove may belong in an authorized sandbox Earth, but should not silently become the default depiction of Barton Creek.

* **Tree families.** Make a few reusable, original tree archetypes: a broad, asymmetric live-oak-like crown with lateral branches and visible sky gaps; an upright dense juniper/cedar-like silhouette; and a smaller understory form. Use deterministic variations of trunk lean, branch spread, crown rotation, age, and canopy tint. First verify silhouette in gray material at walking height. Add leaf-cluster cards or sculpted tufts only where they improve the silhouette and near-field depth. Avoid spheres with random noise pasted on top.
* **Limestone and terrain.** Keep the measured landform. Give close slopes and ledges recognizable layered, fractured limestone shapes and soil seams, then fade to simpler terrain materials at distance. Use a small, regionally plausible palette of warm pale rock, darker joint/shadow, reddish-brown trail soil, and muted vegetation. Put the highest-frequency marks at trail and creek edges, not across every square meter.
* **Creek.** Replace the flat color ribbon in the study with a bounded creek-edge system: bank geometry, rock/soil transitions, shallow/deep color variation, limited surface highlights, and clear contacts with paths and ledges. Do not claim hydrological fidelity or invent a water level from an undocumented photo.
* **Built and edited objects.** Preserve recognizable mall massing. Give modern walls, glazing, paths, and player-built components controlled bevels and material variation. A path, generated join, and platform must share stone course direction, contact shading, and lighting after each edit and after reload. The style pin and semantic source parts remain the authority; decorative meshes regenerate from them.
* **Color and light.** Start from the existing restrained-natural Greenbelt recipe. Seek the references' warm/cool contrast and selective saturation through a coherent daylight scene, shadow color, sky haze, and local material variation. Test the effect in neutral daylight as well as a warm late-day preset; it must still read as Barton Creek without universal pink trees, blanket bloom, or a screen-space paint filter.

## Implementable art pass

1. **One hero cluster.** Author one live-oak-like tree, one juniper/cedar-like tree, one limestone ledge, and one small creek-bank segment as original reusable source assets. Give each a close and distant representation. Keep roots, branching, rock strata, and creek edge readable from the 1.67 m camera before adding fine detail.
2. **Material vocabulary.** Use a few shared material families with controlled per-instance color/roughness or vertex-color variation. Start with broad painted color masses; add small, directional marks on bark, stone, soil, and leaf clusters. Test at walking distance and in the construction view. Avoid camera-facing photographic noise that dissolves into shimmer.
3. **Scene integration.** Place the assets against the pinned Greenbelt map and in the actual editable workshop, using seeded placement only where vegetation is illustrative. Keep trail visibility and build controls readable. Regenerate visual joins from editable parts, and compare before edit, after edit, and after reload at the same camera.
4. **Lighting and atmosphere.** Use one controlled sun direction and a sky/ambient fill as the first pass. Introduce modest atmospheric depth and color grading only after geometry and materials read without them. Capture both standard and low profiles. Do not make a high-cost effect essential to the identity.
5. **Streaming and low profile.** Reuse meshes/materials; divide dense vegetation into spatially bounded instance groups so nearby groups can be culled or unloaded. Use authored or imported levels of detail/visibility ranges for large trees and structures. In the low profile retain the same tree silhouettes, stone layering, creek edge and material hierarchy; reduce small leaf/grass instances, distant detail, shadow resolution/effects, and atmosphere cost first. [Godot's MultiMesh guidance](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html) notes that each MultiMesh is culled as a whole, which is why regional groups matter. [Godot's visibility-range guidance](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html) supports different near and far representations. Profile the actual target laptop before raising the existing scene budgets.

The current integrated workshop captures are approximately **5/10 standard and 4.5/10 low** by the prior art assessment. They establish edit/collision integration but show plain sky, flat terrain, broad solid-color surfaces, and rounded tree masses. The next pass should improve the integrated playable view first; a separate beautiful reference scene alone cannot pass the game art gate.

## Paired acceptance review

Capture the **same camera, time of day, source map, and player edit state** at walking height (1.67 m) in standard and low. Include a creek/trail view, a modern built-edge view, and the workshop before/after/reloaded views. Also inspect one overhead editing view. Three reviewers independent of the implementation rate each criterion 1–10, with evidence from the paired captures and a playable walk. The target is at least **8.5/10 in both profiles**, no criterion under 8 in either profile, and the repository's [milestone gate](../quality-gates.md) still applies. These are proposed art-specific criteria, not a claim that current captures pass.

| Criterion (weight) | Standard profile should show | Low profile must retain |
|---|---|---|
| Barton identity and scale (15%) | Recognizable landform/trail/creek and contemporary mall/interchange; natural human-scale distances | Same recognizable geography and real-world proportions |
| Silhouette and vegetation (20%) | Distinct oak-like, juniper/cedar-like, and understory forms with asymmetry, branch gaps, layered crowns | The same archetypes and canopy hierarchy with fewer fine clusters, without reverting to generic spheres |
| Materials and selective detail (15%) | Coherent limestone, bark, soil, glass/metal and water; close detail follows form, distance simplifies | Same material families and color relationships; quieter fine marks rather than flat replacement colors |
| Light, color and atmosphere (15%) | Warm/cool relationships, readable shadows, atmospheric depth without blown highlights | Readable depth and focal hierarchy even with cheaper shadows/atmosphere |
| Terrain, creek and contact (15%) | Water has banks and depth cues; trail and rocks meet terrain; no floating or sharply pasted seams | Bank and contact geometry remain legible with simpler highlights/instances |
| Editable style continuity (10%) | Player path/platform/join share treatment after change and reload; modern additions fit visual language | Same edit identities, joins and collision; no visual state changes caused by lowering graphics quality |
| Motion, readability and cost (10%) | Walk and edit without foliage shimmer or obscured controls; benchmark representative camera movement | Readable controls and silhouettes while meeting the named low-device frame, memory, streaming and residency gates |

Do not use a still-image score to certify performance or editability. Record the named device, frame-time distribution, loading peaks, and memory residency separately. If the tree crowns look painterly only from one angle or the low profile loses their species identity, keep the art gate open even if one hero screenshot is attractive.
