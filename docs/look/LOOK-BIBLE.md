# Look bible: storybook painterly

**Status: draft (Run 1, Look lane).** The founder's direction of 6 October is recorded below: **style first, in the spirit of Tiny Glade; material media later**, and camera angles that make the scene read as artistic. The founder has written notes on some reference frames and is still writing the rest. The rubric, cameras and budgets apply now. Reviewers score against this file; only the founder passes the look gate.

Preset: `game/styles/storybook_painterly/v1.json` (status `draft`). Review cameras: `tools/look/review_cameras.json`. Captures: `docs/look/reviews/`.

## Style first, Tiny Glade spirit; media later

The founder's decision (6 October, later the same evening as the media decision): **do not pick a first material medium yet.** The player will later choose a medium (felt, stone, clay, yarn, cardboard and so on) in which every surface manifests, and each medium will need research into real handcrafted work. That comes after one artistic style is settled.

The style comes first, and it is **one stylized storybook style in the spirit of Tiny Glade**: cozy and fanciful (the words the founder wants players to use), cottage-core, handmade, never photorealistic. Concretely:

- **Stylized, not photographic.** Gently desaturated pastel colour, painted rather than photographed surfaces, soft rounded forms, and every leaf, flower and shingle reading as a distinct object (Tiny Glade's procedural detail; L5 work for later runs).
- **Light that tells the hour.** Warm twilight and sunset pastels (orange, gold and light blue), deep blue nights with warm lamps, golden afternoons. Fairy lights and glowing windows give a handcrafted warmth (later runs).
- **Light only from real sources.** Windows and lamps, no "lid lifted off" sun. Closing the blinds or switching off the lights darkens the room, and changing the lights at 10 cm is a puzzle. See "Light from real sources" below.
- **Camera angles that read as art.** High-angle and isometric views with a tilt-shift miniature feel; every view gently blurs the foreground and the background, keeping the player and companion crisp. See "Cameras and depth of field" below.
- **Architecture styles are later player options**: medieval and cottage (`Tiny Glade 1.png`), urban (`Titl Shift 2.png`, `Titl Shift 3.png`), modern and sci-fi (`Scifi artistic.png`).
- **Avatars are felt figurines**, soft and pliable (a later packet; three options each for head, torso, arms and legs, mostly colour variations).

## The target in one paragraph

A real room, photographed by the player, that looks like a **handmade toy diorama on a tabletop**, seen by a 10 cm person. The founder's notes set the qualities: Tiny Glade's art style as the target; soft, slightly wonky, rounded forms instead of sharp sterile ones; subtle tactile texture on wood, brick and fabric so the world feels made by hand; warm, dynamic light with bounce, warm ambient and rich shadow contrast that shifts with the time of day; a soothing, desaturated palette that follows the real calendar (golden summer, autumn oranges and reds, icy winter blues) and the real clock (darker, bluer nights with deeper shadows, warm pastel twilights); and a high-quality depth of field like tilt-shift photography, where everything in reach stays crisp, far things soften, and anything between the camera and the character blurs away so you can see through it. Detail should appear as the camera comes closer rather than everywhere at once.

## Scoring rubric

A reviewer scores each criterion 0 to 10 from the review cameras, writes at least one specific critique per criterion (what is wrong, where, compared with which reference), and names the change that would raise it. The overall score is the weighted mean. The target is **8.5**, the same bar as the earlier art work. A score is evidence for the founder, not a pass.

| Criterion | Weight | Judge it on | 3: blockout | 6: on the way | 8.5: target | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Palette and desaturation | 1.0 | Ceiling corner, low corner, isometric room | One hue family everywhere, or raw saturated primaries | Calm saturation, but large areas of one beige or brown | Soothing, gently desaturated; warm and cool families both present; accents (rug, book, avatars) sing without shouting; season reads | Could be mistaken for a Tiny Glade frame's palette |
| Warm key against cool shadow | 1.2 | All | Flat light; shade is grey or brown | Key and shade differ in value but both are warm | Sunlit planes clearly warm; shade and cast shadows clearly cool and never black; bounce warms surfaces near lit floor | Light alone tells the hour |
| Edge softness and form | 0.8 | Over the shoulder, companion, player eye | Hard CG edges, perfect boxes | Edges catch light but read machined | Box props read as rounded, slightly worn, hand-cut objects; no razor silhouettes at play distance | Every silhouette feels sculpted |
| Material handmade-ness per role | 1.2 | Over the shoulder, companion, player eye | Flat colour or photo noise | Visible marks that read as noise or as the wrong material | Each role reads as itself and as hand-made (see the table below): painted plaster, planks, woven rug, book pages, taped carton, rubber | A viewer wants to touch it |
| Clutter density | 0.6 | Ceiling corner, low corner | Empty box | Props spaced like a showroom | A lived-in diorama: small things in reach, quiet areas around paths | Tiny Glade 6 levels of intent |
| Depth of field | 1.0 | All | None, or everything blurred | Blur present but the reach is soft or the far room is crisp | Player's reach crisp; far room soft; near occluders melt; a high view reads as tilt-shift | Indistinguishable from good macro photography |
| Diorama feel | 1.2 | Ceiling corner, over the shoulder, high-angle diorama, isometric room | A level in an engine | A small room, but life-size in feeling | Reads instantly as a miniature, handmade world on a tabletop | Reads as a photograph of a real handmade model |

Clutter density is scored but weighted low in Run 1: the placeholder room has five primitive props by design, and clutter, trim and vines are L5 (Run 3). Scores on the five baseline cameras stay comparable across rounds; the two art-direction cameras are scored separately.

## What "handmade" means per material role

Run 1 implements these in `game/shaders/painterly_material.gdshader` through `MaterialLibrary`, with the per-role numbers in the preset's `materials.roles` and the role's pattern and brush marks in `x_look_role_marks`. All marks live in object space (they travel with an object) and fade out before they alias, so finer marks appear only as the camera comes close. These treatments belong to the style, not to a medium; a medium (felt, clay, cardboard) will later replace or retexture them.

| Role | Target | Run 1 treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `painted_wall`, `plaster`, `wallpaper` | Hand-rolled paint over plaster: soft clouds of value and temperature, faint roller direction, never a photo texture | Domain-warped broad washes and soft strokes, warm where light and cool where dark; light stroke normals |
| `wood`, `wood_painted` | Planks laid by hand: visible seams and butt joints, each plank its own tone, grain that follows the long side, end grain different from face grain | Planks along an object's longest side, per-plank tone, staggered joints, ring streaks, rounded worn edges |
| `fabric`, `carpet` | Woven, soft, slightly fuzzy; a rug has a border | Fine weave that fades with distance, sheen at grazing angles, a border band in the palette accent on rugs |
| `paper` | A book or stack: cover boards and cream page edges | Cream page edges with fine page lines on three sides, covers top, bottom and spine |
| `cardboard` | A taped carton: flaps meeting in a seam, packing tape over it, fluting on the sides, scuffed corners | Seam and tape across the top and down two sides, faint flutes, heavy edge wear |
| `rubber` | Matte, dense, flecked | Sparse round flecks, high roughness |
| `metal`, `metal_painted` | Brushed or painted metal, worn bright at edges | Brushed streaks along the long side, low softening |
| `plastic`, `ceramic`, `glass`, `leather` | Smooth, a little glaze, soft highlights | Smooth with gentle mottling and a stronger highlight |
| `stone`, `concrete`, `brick` | Pitted, mottled, model-making plaster | Pores and specks; concrete may add the painted ground texture |

Every role also gets: a glaze toward its tint (hue only, the room's base colour keeps its lightness), albedo softening toward a gentle pastel, stroke normals, and, on box props, shading-only rounded edges with lighter worn paint (real bevels and wobble are L4, Run 2). Lighting on these materials uses a wrapped diffuse with a narrow warm band at the terminator and a cool fill inside the key light's cast shadows.

## Light, time and season

- **The diorama key (until the test room has a window).** The key light lights the room as if the lid were lifted off the box: the shell casts no key shadows, props and avatars do. It sits at the preset's elevation and azimuth at the default hour (16:30) on the reference day and turns 15 degrees an hour along a day arc that never climbs above 60 degrees; at night it becomes a dim moon. The founder has decided that light comes only from real sources (windows and lamps), so this key is temporary: it stays until the test room has a window, then the window plan below replaces it.
- **Sky and bounce.** The cool ambient colour is the sky seen through the open lid. VoxelGI, baked to the room bounds at load, carries the warm floor bounce; walls and ceiling stay out of the bake so they do not block the lid, but they receive its light.
- **The real clock by default.** Time of day follows the computer's clock and seasons follow its calendar (northern hemisphere by default). The look re-reads the clock every 10 seconds; a moment whose grade is not cached builds it off the main thread. For reviews, playtests and tests the clock is deterministic: review captures pin it from `tools/look/review_cameras.json` (16:30 on 6 October), code pins it with `LookDirector.SetClock`, and a player or reviewer can pin it with `--look-clock=HH:MM` and `--look-date=YYYY-MM-DD` after `--` on the command line, or the `ENFRACTAL_LOOK_CLOCK` and `ENFRACTAL_LOOK_DATE` environment variables. `LookDirector.ClockNote` says which applies.
- **One reference day, stretched by the season.** The time keys describe one reference day (sunrise about 05:35, sunset about 19:05): deep blue night, peach dawn, warm white noon, golden afternoon at 16:30, orange-gold sunset under a light-blue sky, blue hour, night. The season sets the hours of daylight (`x_look_seasons.day_length_h`: 9.5 in winter, 13 in spring, 15 in summer, 11 in autumn, blended between the mid-season days) around the reference noon, so at six in the evening it is night in winter, sunset light in autumn and a golden afternoon in summer. The review moment (16:30 on 6 October) reads the reference day at about 17:28: late golden light.
- **Stronger swings (6 October).** Nights fall to 2% of the afternoon key with a deep blue ambient a fifth of midday's, and room lamps glow three and a half times brighter at night than by day. The season grades differ clearly: icy blue winter, fresh green spring, golden summer, orange-red autumn, with the season tint at 0.36 and the season colouring the sunlight at 0.4. The season tint fades out of the shadows (`season_tint_shadow_fade`), so shade stays cool against a warm season.
- **What the look test checks at every minute of four days, one per season.** Light changes smoothly; it rises once from its darkest minute, which falls in the night, to its brightest and falls once back (so 2 a.m. is darker than 9 p.m.); the key is pure sun whenever daylight is at least half; it swings from sun to moon only while the light is dim, so it never flips at full strength; it never shines from below the horizon; and the real day maps continuously onto the reference day. Sunrise and sunset are where daylight crosses `x_look_sun.moon_daylight`.
- **Grade.** After tone mapping, a 3D LUT applies warmth, the season tint, cool shadows and warm highlights from the palette, the palette saturation, night, and contrast. LUTs are cached by their (quantized) inputs, and a moment the cache has not seen builds off the main thread, so following the clock never stalls a frame for the 13 to 18 ms one LUT takes.
- **Every look number is in the preset.** Beyond the schema fields, the preset's `x_look_*` blocks hold every number the look uses: role brush marks and patterns (`x_look_role_marks`), the shader's shared paint numbers (`x_look_paint`), shadows, SSAO, glow, VoxelGI, lamps, the sun's path, seasons and day lengths, grade, depth of field and the grain and vignette shape. The code keeps defaults only for presets written before these blocks; the look test fails if the shipped preset leaves any number to them, so a pinned preset version reproduces its look.
- **Renderer fallback.** When the machine renders with something other than the preset's renderer (for example Compatibility after a Vulkan failure), the look logs a `LOOK WARNING`, the review harness records it and fails the capture, and `LookDirector.PlayerNotice` carries a sentence for the HUD. It is never silent.

## Light from real sources (the plan once the room has a window)

The founder: light comes only from real sources, windows and lamps; closing the blinds or switching off the lights darkens the room. The test room needs a window first; its builder belongs to the integrator, and the Look lane's change request specifies it: a window in the south wall, 1.2 m wide and 1.5 m tall, its sill 0.5 m above the floor, centred 1.0 m west of the room's centre line, with a window light hint just outside it and a sun hint. Then the look lights the room like this:

- **Sun through the window.** The key becomes the sun on the real clock's path, coming from the room's south-east in the morning, south at noon and south-west to west in the afternoon (the test room's north is -Z), and **the shell casts its shadows**: only what the window lets through lands as a warm patch on the floor, the rug and the avatars, moving across the room through the day. At 16:30 in October the patch falls across the rug and the spawn from the south-west, with long elongated shadows (`Tiny Glade 1.png`'s light placement).
- **Sky through the window.** A soft spot light just outside the opening, aimed in, carries the cool sky colour of the hour (the time keys' ambient colour), with soft-edged window-frame shadows.
- **A closed room.** Walls and ceiling join the VoxelGI bake as an interior, so the sun patch bounces warm light around the room; the environment's ambient falls to a small fraction, so the room is dark where the window does not reach and at night.
- **Lamps.** The ceiling lamp, later table lamps and fairy lights, glow warmer and brighter at night and are the main light after dusk.
- **Blinds and switches.** An opening's `state` (open or closed) and a lamp's on/off become room state changed through commands; the look follows them. The physical puzzles of operating them at 10 cm are Play-lane work.
- **Budget.** Three shadowed lights (sun, sky spot, lamp) within the preset's four.

## Cameras and depth of field

The founder's direction: the player chooses over-the-shoulder, first person, or an **isometric or high-angle view** that shows more of the world around the character, and **every view gently blurs the foreground and background**. While moving, focus follows the player and the companion; while building and zoomed out, focus follows the mouse or a free camera the player controls.

- **Focus follows the avatars.** Focus sits on the player's body. When the companion is within `x_look_dof.companion_follow_m` (0.8 m) of the player, the crisp band stretches to keep it crisp too, so the two read as one subject. From the player's own eye the focus sits just beyond reach and the reach (`eye_crisp_body_heights`, 1.5 body heights) stays crisp; anything nearer than 8 cm blurs.
- **Focus follows the cursor while building.** `LookDirector.FocusOverride` takes a world point (the build camera's cursor hit or a free camera's target); while it is set, every unframed camera focuses there. The preset's `"cursor"` focus mode uses it too.
- **Tilt-shift from above.** Looking down on the room narrows the crisp band (`tilt_band_narrowing`), shortens the blur ramps (`tilt_transition_shortening`) and strengthens the blur (`tilt_amount_gain`), scaled by `camera.tilt_shift.strength` (0.8). From 45 degrees up and 1.1 m away the crisp band is about 30 cm deep around the avatars, the floor at the bottom of the frame sits in front of it and the far room at the top is deep in the blur: the tabletop-miniature read. The player's level eye view is unchanged.
- **Finish.** A static, paper-like grain and a light vignette (`post.grain`, `post.vignette`) finish the frame.

The player's eye height (8.7 cm) is the Run 1 target profile. The first five are the Run 1 baseline and never change, because the scoring history depends on them; the last two were added for the 6 October art direction.

| Id | Where | Judges |

| `over_shoulder` | Behind and above the player's left shoulder | The default play view: avatar against room, contact shadows, depth of field |

| `iso_room` | High by the north wall, 2.7 m from the avatars, about 35 degrees down (the isometric angle), 30-degree lens | The isometric overview: the whole room as an artistic handmade diorama around the avatars |

The look test checks the framing: the five baseline cameras are unchanged, every camera stands inside the room looking at its focus, and from the two art-direction cameras both avatars sit inside the crisp band while the floor at the bottom of the frame is nearer than the band and the top of the frame is deep in the far blur.

Capture (on the founder's machine, never while the game is open; a Godot window appears briefly):

```powershell
pwsh -NoProfile -File tools/look/capture-look.ps1 -Label after -OutDir docs/look/reviews/run1/after -Sweep
pwsh -NoProfile -File tools/look/capture-look.ps1 -Label before -OutDir docs/look/reviews/run1/before -Baseline <commit>
```

Each run writes one 1920 × 1080 PNG per camera and `timings.json` (frame time with vsync off, GPU time, draw calls, engine, renderer, adapter, preset hash, the look's self-check and a pixel check of the grain and vignette). `-Sweep` adds a contact sheet of one camera across the seasons and hours named in the cameras file (`sweep`: `diorama_high`, mid-season days, 07:00, 12:00, 16:30, 18:00 and 21:00). The script refuses to start while a game window titled "EnFractal" is open, waits for the card to go quiet, records GPU memory during the run, and retries when another job ran meanwhile. The offscreen review target was checked against rendering in the window itself: identical pixels (worst difference 0.3/255) and the same frame times.

## Budgets

The preset budget is **16.7 ms per frame at 1920 × 1080 on an NVIDIA RTX 2070 SUPER (8 GB)**, at most four shadowed lights. Reviews report p50, p95 and p99 frame time per camera. The VoxelGI bake runs once at room load (under 0.5 s for the test room) and is not part of the frame budget.

## Not in Run 1

- **Material media** (felt, clay, yarn, cardboard and so on): deferred by the founder until the style is settled; each needs research into real handcrafted work in that medium.
- **L4 handmade geometry**: real bevels, slight wobble and softened silhouettes on meshes. Run 1 fakes rounded edges in shading only.
- **L5 procedural charm**: vines, moss, trim, crumbs, clutter, leaves, flowers and shingles as distinct objects, growing on built and transformed surfaces, densest near edges and in corners, quiet around paths and interaction points. The preset's `charm` block is reserved for it and stays disabled.
- **L6 offline restyle** of captured assets' textures.
- **Felt figurine avatars** (later packet).
- **Kuwahara and outline post effects**: in the schema, disabled in the preset, not implemented.
- **A Compatibility fallback**: none; add one only if a measured device requirement appears.
