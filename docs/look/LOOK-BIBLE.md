# Look bible: storybook painterly

**Status: draft (Run 1, Look lane).** The founder's direction of 6 October is recorded below: **style first, in the spirit of Tiny Glade; material media later**, and camera angles that make the scene read as artistic. The founder has written notes on some reference frames and is still writing the rest. The rubric, cameras and budgets apply now. Reviewers score against this file; only the founder passes the look gate.

Preset: `game/styles/storybook_painterly/v1.json` (status `draft`). Review cameras: `tools/look/review_cameras.json`. Captures: `docs/look/reviews/`.

## Style first, Tiny Glade spirit; media later

The founder's decision (6 October, later the same evening as the media decision): **do not pick a first material medium yet.** The player will later choose a medium (felt, stone, clay, yarn, cardboard and so on) in which every surface manifests, and each medium will need research into real handcrafted work. That comes after one artistic style is settled.

The style comes first, and it is **one stylized storybook style in the spirit of Tiny Glade**: cozy and fanciful (the words the founder wants players to use), cottage-core, handmade, never photorealistic. Concretely:

- **Stylized, not photographic.** Gently desaturated pastel colour, painted rather than photographed surfaces, soft rounded forms, and every leaf, flower and shingle reading as a distinct object (Tiny Glade's procedural detail; L5 work for later runs).
- **Light that tells the hour.** Warm twilight and sunset pastels (orange, gold and light blue), deep blue nights with warm lamps, golden afternoons. Fairy lights and glowing windows give a handcrafted warmth (later runs).
- **Light only from real sources.** The sun through the windows, the sky through them, and the lamps; no "lid lifted off" sun. Closing the blinds or switching off the lights darkens the room, and changing the lights at 10 cm is a puzzle. See "Light from real sources" below.
- **Camera angles that read as art.** High-angle and isometric views with a tilt-shift miniature feel; every view gently blurs the foreground and the background, keeping the player and companion crisp. See "Cameras and depth of field" below.
- **Architecture styles are later player options**: medieval and cottage (`Tiny Glade 1.png`), urban (`Titl Shift 2.png`, `Titl Shift 3.png`), modern and sci-fi (`Scifi artistic.png`).
- **Avatars are felt figurines**, soft and pliable (a later packet; three options each for head, torso, arms and legs, mostly colour variations).

## The target in one paragraph

A real room, photographed by the player, that looks like a **handmade toy diorama on a tabletop**, seen by a 10 cm person. The founder's notes set the qualities: Tiny Glade's art style as the target; soft, slightly wonky, rounded forms instead of sharp sterile ones; subtle tactile texture on wood, brick and fabric so the world feels made by hand; warm, dynamic light with bounce, warm ambient and rich shadow contrast that shifts with the time of day; a soothing, desaturated palette that follows the real calendar (golden summer, autumn oranges and reds, icy winter blues) and the real clock (darker, bluer nights with deeper shadows, warm pastel twilights); and a high-quality depth of field like tilt-shift photography, where everything in reach stays crisp, far things soften, and anything between the camera and the character blurs away so you can see through it. Detail should appear as the camera comes closer rather than everywhere at once.

## Scoring rubric

A reviewer scores each criterion 0 to 10 from the review cameras, writes at least one specific critique per criterion (what is wrong, where, compared with which reference), and names the change that would raise it. The overall score is the weighted mean. The target is **8.5**, the same bar as the earlier art work. A score is evidence for the founder, not a pass.

| Criterion | Weight | Judge it on | 3: blockout | 6: on the way | 8.5: target | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Palette and desaturation | 1.0 | Ceiling corner, low corner, isometric room | One hue family everywhere, or raw saturated primaries | Calm saturation, but large areas of one beige or brown | Soothing, gently desaturated; warm and cool families both present; accents (rug, book, avatars) sing without shouting; season reads | Could be mistaken for a Tiny Glade frame's palette |
| Warm key against cool shadow | 1.2 | All | Flat light; shade is grey or brown | Key and shade differ in value but both are warm | Sunlit planes clearly warm; shade and cast shadows clearly cool and never black; bounce warms surfaces near lit floor | Light alone tells the hour |
| Edge softness and form | 0.8 | Over the shoulder, companion, player eye | Hard CG edges, perfect boxes | Edges catch light but read machined | Box props read as rounded, slightly worn, hand-cut objects; no razor silhouettes at play distance | Every silhouette feels sculpted |
| Material handmade-ness per role | 1.2 | Over the shoulder, companion, player eye | Flat colour or photo noise | Visible marks that read as noise or as the wrong material | Each role reads as itself and as hand-made (see the table below): painted plaster, planks, woven rug, book pages, taped carton, rubber | A viewer wants to touch it |
| Clutter density | 0.6 | Ceiling corner, low corner | Empty box | Props spaced like a showroom | A lived-in diorama: small things in reach, quiet areas around paths | Tiny Glade 6 levels of intent |
| Depth of field | 1.0 | All | None, or everything blurred | Blur present but the reach is soft or the far room is crisp | Player's reach crisp; far room soft; near occluders melt; a high view reads as tilt-shift | Indistinguishable from good macro photography |
| Diorama feel | 1.2 | Ceiling corner, over the shoulder, high-angle diorama, isometric room | A level in an engine | A small room, but life-size in feeling | Reads instantly as a miniature, handmade world on a tabletop | Reads as a photograph of a real handmade model |

Clutter density is scored but weighted low in Run 1: the placeholder room has five primitive props by design, and clutter, trim and vines are L5 (Run 3). Scores on the five baseline cameras stay comparable across rounds; the two art-direction cameras are scored separately.

## What "handmade" means per material role

Run 1 implements these in `game/shaders/painterly_material.gdshader` through `MaterialLibrary`, with the per-role numbers in the preset's `materials.roles` and the role's pattern and brush marks in `x_look_role_marks`. All marks live in object space (they travel with an object) and fade out before they alias, so finer marks appear only as the camera comes close. These treatments belong to the style, not to a medium; a medium (felt, clay, cardboard) will later replace or retexture them.

| Role | Target | Run 1 treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `painted_wall`, `plaster`, `wallpaper` | Hand-rolled paint over plaster: soft clouds of value and temperature, faint roller direction, never a photo texture | Domain-warped broad washes and soft strokes, warm where light and cool where dark; light stroke normals |
| `wood`, `wood_painted` | Planks laid by hand: visible seams and butt joints, each plank its own tone, grain that follows the long side, end grain different from face grain | Planks along an object's longest side, per-plank tone, staggered joints, ring streaks, rounded worn edges |
| `fabric`, `carpet` | Woven, soft, slightly fuzzy; a rug has a border | Fine weave that fades with distance, sheen at grazing angles, a border band in the palette accent on rugs |
| `paper` | A book or stack: cover boards and cream page edges | Cream page edges with fine page lines on three sides, covers top, bottom and spine |
| `cardboard` | A taped carton: flaps meeting in a seam, packing tape over it, fluting on the sides, scuffed corners | Seam and tape across the top and down two sides, faint flutes, heavy edge wear |
| `rubber` | Matte, dense, flecked | Sparse round flecks, high roughness |
| `metal`, `metal_painted` | Brushed or painted metal, worn bright at edges | Brushed streaks along the long side, low softening |
| `plastic`, `ceramic`, `glass`, `leather` | Smooth, a little glaze, soft highlights | Smooth with gentle mottling and a stronger highlight |
| `stone`, `concrete`, `brick` | Pitted, mottled, model-making plaster | Pores and specks; concrete may add the painted ground texture |

Every role also gets: a glaze toward its tint where the preset gives one (hue only, the room's base colour keeps its lightness; round 2 dropped the wood and painted-wall tints and lowered the glaze to 0.12, so a captured or hand-picked palette reads as itself), albedo softening toward a gentle pastel, stroke normals, and, on box props, shading-only rounded edges with lighter worn paint (real bevels and wobble are L4, Run 2). Lighting on these materials uses a wrapped diffuse with a narrow warm band at the terminator, scaled by the light's own warmth, so the sun and lamps get it and the cool sky fill and the moon do not. Nothing adds light inside a cast shadow: shade is lit by the window's fill and the bounce.

## Light from real sources

The founder (6 October): light comes only from real sources. The test room has a west window (the founder chose west for sunset light; the room's -Z is north), and the look lights it like this. Three shadowed lights in all: the sun, the window's sky fill and the ceiling lamp (`budgets.max_shadowed_lights` 3).

- **The sun, only through the window.** The key is the real sun (`x_look_key_mode` `sun`) on a solar model, and the room's shell casts its shadows like everything else, so direct sun lands only where the window lets it through: a gold patch that crosses the floor through the afternoon, reaching the rug and the spawns on spring and summer evenings, with long shadows behind every prop and both avatars (`Tiny Glade 1.png`'s light). In the morning the sun is in the east and no direct sun enters. A room without a `sun` light hint gets no direct sun at all.
- **The sky through the window.** Each window light hint becomes a soft, shadowed spot light standing `window_standoff_m` (0.6 m) outside the opening and `window_light_size_m` (0.6 m) across, aimed in along the hint's direction. Its colour is the sky of the hour (the time keys' ambient colour, through the window's own colour) at `sky_fill_saturation` (0.55) of its hue, a light blue rather than a saturated one; its brightness follows the sky's.
- **A closed interior.** Floor, walls and ceiling are baked into VoxelGI as an interior, so what comes through the window bounces round the room (VoxelGI energy 1.0, propagation 0.6). Inside an interior VoxelGI volume the environment's ambient light has no effect (measured: identical frames at ambient 0 and 1); `x_look_gi.environment_ambient_scale` (0.15) only matters where VoxelGI is missing, such as a renderer fallback.
- **Lamps are lamps.** A lamp shines at one constant brightness when it is on. Until room state carries switches, lamps switch themselves on when daylight falls below `switch_on_below_daylight` (0.25, around sunset); `LookDirector.SetLamps(true/false/null)`, `--look-lamps=on|off|auto` or `ENFRACTAL_LOOK_LAMPS` pin them. The style warms captured lamp colours halfway toward `lamp_tint` (#ffb878): storybook lamps glow warmer than life.
- **Nights at moonlight level.** The founder: dark, but you can make out shapes. The moon stands 35 degrees up outside the room's brightest window, so cool moonlight falls in through it; it takes over from the sun only during civil twilight (the sun between 0 and -6 degrees), while the light is dim. With the lamps off at 02:00 the room is dark blue with every shape readable (frame mean luma about 0.15); with the lamps on it is warm. The night grade (bluer, calmer, slightly deeper) applies in full under moonlight and only `night_with_lamps` (10%) under lamplight, so lamplight keeps its warmth.
- **Shadows that ground 10 cm bodies.** The sun's shadow is filtered (`shadow_blur` 1.55), not softened by PCSS: at the old 2.4 degrees of angular size PCSS erased the shadows of the avatars and the props entirely (the window frame's survived), and cost about 2 ms a frame. Biases are small and separate for the sun (`key_bias` 0.02, `key_normal_bias` 0.6) and the positional lights (`lamp_bias` 0.02, `lamp_normal_bias` 0.6); zero bias showed acne on the rug. The sun's shadow covers 1.1 room diagonals in two splits, the first over 30% of it. With zero bias the companion's lamp shadow still starts below its base, so the remaining hover is the body's height above the floor, not the light.
- **Blinds and switches** (later): an opening's `state` and a lamp's on/off become room state changed through commands, and the look follows them. Operating them at 10 cm is Play-lane work.

## Time and season

- **The sun follows a solar model.** Its elevation and compass bearing come from the latitude, the date and the local standard clock time (NOAA's declination and equation-of-time series; sunrise and sunset where the sun's centre crosses -0.833 degrees, the almanac's definition), so the day is as long as it really is on every date. The preset's `x_look_sun` holds the defaults: latitude 30 degrees north (the founder's; no longitude or place is recorded), the room's -Z axis facing north (`neg_z_bearing_deg` 0, so -X is west), mean solar noon at 12:00 standard time. At 30 degrees north a day lasts 10.2 h at the December solstice, 12.1 h at the equinoxes and 14.1 h at the June solstice. A room's own latitude and orientation belong in the room contract later (proposed in the Look lane's round 2 report).
- **The real clock by default, read as standard time.** Time of day follows the computer's clock and seasons its calendar. While the machine's time zone is in daylight saving the clock is read an hour earlier (`real_clock_daylight_saving`), so the sun stays on standard time. The look re-reads the clock every 10 seconds; a moment whose grade is not cached builds it off the main thread. For reviews, playtests and tests the clock is deterministic and always standard time: review captures pin it from `tools/look/review_cameras.json` (16:30 on 6 October), code pins it with `LookDirector.SetClock`, and a player or reviewer can pin it with `--look-clock=HH:MM` and `--look-date=YYYY-MM-DD` after `--`, or `ENFRACTAL_LOOK_CLOCK` and `ENFRACTAL_LOOK_DATE`. `LookDirector.ClockNote` says which applies.
- **The time keys colour the day.** The keys describe one reference day (its sunrise and sunset are where their daylight crosses `reference_daylight`); the real sunrise and sunset are mapped onto them, linearly through the day and the night, so the keys' gold afternoon and orange sunset always fall just before the real sunset, whatever the season. Through the golden hour the sun is gold and turns orange only near sunset; the sky colours are light blue, not lavender. At the review moment (16:30 on 6 October) the sun stands 14 degrees up in the west-south-west and lands north of the spawns.
- **Warmth from the sunlight, not from a tint.** The founder: reduce the global orange cast so the room's greens, blues, creams and pinks read as themselves. The palette's warmth is 0, the season grades' warmth is 0 (winter -0.15), the season tint acts at 0.09 (was 0.36) and the highlights are near neutral. Seasons now show mostly in the sunlight (its colour, at `light_strength` 0.25 of the season's hue; paler in winter), the day length and the sun's height, plus saturation (winter 0.78). Shade stays cool through the palette's blue-grey shadow tint (#5a6480 at 0.5).
- **Grade.** After tone mapping, a 3D LUT applies warmth, the season tint, cool shadows and nearly neutral highlights, the palette saturation, night, and contrast. LUTs are cached by their (quantized) inputs, and a moment the cache has not seen builds off the main thread.
- **What the look test checks.** At every minute of four days, one per season: light changes smoothly and rises once from its darkest minute (at night) to its brightest and falls once back; the key comes from the real sun's direction whenever the sun is up and moves only as fast as the sun does while bright; it swings to the moon only while dim and never shines from below the horizon. The solar model: day lengths at 30 degrees north on the solstices and equinoxes, through the year, noon elevations, sunrise bearings, other latitudes, and the standard-time reading of the clock. Real sources: physics rays from the built room show direct sun reaching the floor only through the window at the review moment and on a summer evening; every shell part casts the sun's shadow; nights at moonlight level with the lamps' switch; a cool light-blue fill and a warm sun through the golden hour; and the grade tints the room's own colours by less than a just-noticeable step (0.015 in Oklab).
- **Every look number is in the preset.** Beyond the schema fields, the preset's `x_look_*` blocks hold every number the look uses. The code keeps defaults only for presets written before these blocks; the look test fails if the shipped preset leaves any number to them. `renderer.shadows.tint` is read but not used while light comes only from real sources: shade takes the colour of the light that reaches it.
- **Renderer fallback.** When the machine renders with something other than the preset's renderer (for example Compatibility after a Vulkan failure), the look logs a `LOOK WARNING`, the review harness records it and fails the capture, and `LookDirector.PlayerNotice` carries a sentence for the HUD. It is never silent.

## Cameras and depth of field

The founder's direction: the player chooses over-the-shoulder, first person, or an **isometric or high-angle view** that shows more of the world around the character, and **every view gently blurs the foreground and background**. While moving, focus follows the player and the companion; while building and zoomed out, focus follows the mouse or a free camera the player controls.

- **Focus follows the avatars.** Focus sits on the player's body. When the companion is within `x_look_dof.companion_follow_m` (0.8 m) of the player, the crisp band stretches to keep it crisp too, so the two read as one subject. From the player's own eye the focus sits just beyond reach and the reach (`eye_crisp_body_heights`, 1.5 body heights) stays crisp; anything nearer than 8 cm blurs.
- **Focus follows the cursor while building.** `LookDirector.FocusOverride` takes a world point (the build camera's cursor hit or a free camera's target); while it is set, every unframed camera focuses there. The preset's `"cursor"` focus mode uses it too.
- **Tilt-shift from above.** Looking down on the room narrows the crisp band (`tilt_band_narrowing`), shortens the blur ramps (`tilt_transition_shortening`) and strengthens the blur (`tilt_amount_gain`), scaled by `camera.tilt_shift.strength` (0.8). From 45 degrees up the crisp band is about 30 cm deep around the avatars, the floor at the bottom of the frame sits in front of it and the far room at the top is deep in the blur: the tabletop-miniature read. The player's level eye view is unchanged.
- **Finish.** A static, paper-like grain and a light vignette (`post.grain`, `post.vignette`) finish the frame.

## Review cameras

All sit at fixed world positions in the test room, independent of the avatars, with a pinned hour (16:30, standard time) and date (6 October, autumn) so rounds compare like with like. The player's eye height (8.7 cm) is the Run 1 target profile. The first five are the Run 1 baseline and never change, because the scoring history depends on them; the last two were added for the 6 October art direction.

| Id | Where | Judges |
|---|---|---|
| `player_eye` | At the player's spawn, 8.7 cm up, looking toward the table | Reach crispness, near blur, how the floor and props read at 10 cm |
| `over_shoulder` | Behind and above the player's left shoulder | The default play view: avatar against room, contact shadows, depth of field |
| `companion` | In front of both avatars, looking back at them | Both characters in one frame, side light, rug and doorstop up close |
| `low_corner` | Floor level in the north-east corner | Long floor perspective, wall bases, warm wall against cool wall |
| `ceiling_corner` | High in the south-west corner looking down | The tabletop-miniature view: palette, key against shade, tilt-shift |
| `diorama_high` | 1.4 m from the avatars, in front and to their left, 45 degrees down, 40-degree lens | The high-angle tilt-shift miniature framed on the avatars: crisp figures and rug, melting foreground and background |
| `iso_room` | High by the north wall, 2.7 m from the avatars, about 35 degrees down (the isometric angle), 30-degree lens | The isometric overview: the whole room as an artistic handmade diorama around the avatars |

The look test checks the framing: the five baseline cameras are unchanged, every camera stands inside the room looking at its focus, and from the two art-direction cameras both avatars sit inside the crisp band while the floor at the bottom of the frame is nearer than the band and the top of the frame is deep in the far blur.

Capture (on the founder's machine, never while the game is open; a Godot window appears briefly):

```powershell
pwsh -NoProfile -File tools/look/capture-look.ps1 -Label after -OutDir docs/look/reviews/run1/after -Sweep
pwsh -NoProfile -File tools/look/capture-look.ps1 -Label before -OutDir docs/look/reviews/run1/before -Baseline <commit>
```

Each run writes one 1920 × 1080 PNG per camera and `timings.json` (frame time with vsync off, GPU time, draw calls, engine, renderer, adapter, preset hash, the look's self-check and a pixel check of the grain and vignette). `-Sweep` adds a contact sheet of one camera across the seasons and hours named in the cameras file (`sweep`: `diorama_high`, mid-season days, 07:00, 12:00, 15:00, 16:30, 18:00 and 21:00). The light checks (`light_checks` in the cameras file) add pixel evidence for real-source light: at the review clock, floor points the physics says see the sun must brighten with it and points behind the walls must not; `night_<camera>_lamps_off.png` and `_lamps_on.png` at 02:00 with their brightness; and close crops of the avatars' feet (`contact_*.png`) under the window's fill (the review clock), the summer-evening sun across the spawns (`summer_sun_*`) and the lamp at night (`lamp_night_*`). `-Style PATH` renders a preset variant for tuning, never a review of record. The script refuses to start while a game window titled "EnFractal" is open, waits for the card to go quiet, records GPU memory during the run, and retries when another job ran meanwhile. The offscreen review target was checked against rendering in the window itself: identical pixels (worst difference 0.3/255) and the same frame times.

## Budgets

The preset budget is **16.7 ms per frame at 1920 × 1080 on an NVIDIA RTX 2070 SUPER (8 GB)**, at most three shadowed lights (sun, window sky fill, lamp). Round 2 measured 10.4 to 11.6 ms p50 and at most 12.4 ms p95 across the seven cameras. Reviews report p50, p95 and p99 frame time per camera. The VoxelGI bake runs once at room load (under 0.5 s for the test room) and is not part of the frame budget.

## Not in Run 1

- **Material media** (felt, clay, yarn, cardboard and so on): deferred by the founder until the style is settled; each needs research into real handcrafted work in that medium.
- **L4 handmade geometry**: real bevels, slight wobble and softened silhouettes on meshes. Run 1 fakes rounded edges in shading only.
- **L5 procedural charm**: vines, moss, trim, crumbs, clutter, leaves, flowers and shingles as distinct objects, growing on built and transformed surfaces, densest near edges and in corners, quiet around paths and interaction points. The preset's `charm` block is reserved for it and stays disabled.
- **L6 offline restyle** of captured assets' textures.
- **Felt figurine avatars** (later packet).
- **Kuwahara and outline post effects**: in the schema, disabled in the preset, not implemented.
- **A Compatibility fallback**: none; add one only if a measured device requirement appears.
