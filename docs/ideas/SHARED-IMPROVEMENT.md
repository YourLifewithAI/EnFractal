# Held for later: how good ideas spread, and how agents meet safely

**Status:** an idea on record, not a plan. Nothing here is built or scheduled. It may become its own project. Recorded 9 October 2026, from the founder's notes during Run 2's seventh session.

## Where it came from

The founder heard a podcast (*The Cognitive Revolution*, 8 October 2026) about salience: how do you find out that a better tool or practice exists? One guest described an agent that compared their week's work with their goals, and imagined sharing that across collaborators to find synergies. The founder asked two questions:
- "How do we aggregate the best ideas and verify along the way that those ideas were fun and/or useful?"
- "How can agents meet each other safely, with proof of ownership, build proof of trust over time, and share ideas safely? This isn't something to build now, and might be its own project, but there's something important there to hold on record."

## Idea 1: good ideas rise, and fun is verified

- **In the studio.** Runs already loop: playtest, then the founder's notes, then agents build, then reviews, then merge, then playtest again. The A/B log and the routing log already verify *who* does a task well. The missing half is the same discipline for *game ideas*. Each change would be written down as a guess ("the kids will want to swim far"), with what happened when the family played it.
- **In the game.** The shared sea is this idea in game form. Distant islands shaped like other players' islands, and the ferryman's chosen journeys from the jetty, show a player that something good exists and let them choose to go there. The islands, creations and characters that players return to, remix or bring friends to could rise in what the sea shows.
- **Fun is verified by what people do, not what anyone says.** The strongest signal so far was behaviour: the kids swam until they hit the wall. The signals worth counting:
  - what a child does again unprompted;
  - what gets copied or remixed;
  - where people travel and come back.
- **Two cautions:**
  - an AI's score never certifies fun (AGENTS.md: "an agent score does not certify the look");
  - time played is not the goal. For a children's game, optimising it drifts toward compulsion. Charm, invention and no punishment stay the bar; numbers inform the founder's judgement and never replace it.
- **A small first step, when wanted:** a local session summary written from game events, such as what was done and for how long (never screens, faces or voices). After each playtest the integrator reads it beside the founder's notes. Sharing across families comes much later, with opt-in and parental consent, because data about children is regulated.

## Idea 2: agents meet safely

Every player brings their own AI (the founder's decision, 6 October). Once islands are shared, players' agents will meet each other's islands, creations and agents. The questions to answer then:

- **Ownership.** How does anyone know which person stands behind an agent, an island or a creation, and that a claim of authorship is true? Every shared thing needs provenance that can't be forged.
- **Trust over time.** Trust should be earned from a track record: contributions that people verified and kept, endorsements from people who are themselves trusted, and time. It must be possible to lose it, revoke it, and recover from a mistake. Many cheap fake identities must not be able to manufacture it.
- **Sharing safely.** What one agent shares with another is **data, never instructions**. The project's rule already says every name, label and sign is untrusted, and the same rule reaches agents. A shared idea is tried somewhere it can do no harm before it touches anything real. A human approves anything that changes their world, and the record shows where each idea came from.
- **Children.** Nothing personal travels. Parents decide what their children's islands and agents may share, and with whom.

## What already points this way

- **Principals with grants:** every action names who did it, and later guests and their companions join with grants (ROOM-SCALE-DIRECTION.md).
- **The companion's boundary:** the principal is assigned, never claimed. Approval is a human click and is never carried in a request. The companion sees no files, shell, URLs or credentials (AGENTS.md, docs/companion/SECURITY.md).
- **The companion's journal:** a selective record of what was done and at whose direction (docs/companion/JOURNAL.md).
- **The studio's own ledger:** the A/B and routing logs in ORCHESTRATION.md are a small working example of trust earned from verified results.
- **The shared sea and the ferryman** (ROOM-TO-LANDSCAPE.md, "Later").

## Before any of this is built

- Single-player is robust, and multiplayer has passed its own gates.
- Research prior art then, without assuming it fits. Starting points to check:
  - signed provenance for media;
  - verifiable credentials;
  - webs of trust and reputation systems that resist fake identities;
  - capability-based security;
  - how children's platforms handle consent and sharing.
