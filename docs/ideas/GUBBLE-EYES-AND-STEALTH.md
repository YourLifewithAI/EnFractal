# Held for later: seeing through the Gubble's eyes, and Gubbles that hide

**Status:** an idea on record, not a plan. Nothing here is scheduled. Recorded 13 October 2026, from the founder's note during the session that started the AI's "eyes" (a picture from the Gubble's view for whichever AI is connected, behind a player's "Let my AI see" switch).

## The founder's note

"Maybe at some point the player could earn the ability to see through their Gubble's eyes. Since the Gubble can't be seen by other players (unless permission is granted for other players to see the Gubble), then the Gubble could act as a spy of sorts. But I think we should assume that, at baseline, other Gubbles can spot other Gubbles. This opens up a lot of different strategies to avoid detection, such as teaching your Gubble how to change shape into local flora or fauna, or true invisibility where it can't be detected by anything else other than its steward. Anyway, that's later stuff."

## What it builds on

- **The Gubble is set apart from the world** (8 October): a ghost only its player can see; animals never react to it. This idea extends that rule to other players and their Gubbles.
- **The AI's eyes** (built from 13 October): the same camera rig, from the Gubble's eye, could become a view the player earns.
- **Growth by discovery** (the magic design): seeing through the Gubble, disguise and invisibility read naturally as abilities learned by play, with rules per island and mode.

## The rules it implies

- Who can see a Gubble: its steward always; another Gubble by default; other players only with the steward's permission.
- Ways to avoid being spotted: a disguise as local plants or animals; true invisibility to everyone but the steward.
- These are per-viewer visibility rules. The eyes' camera is built with per-viewer visibility (the camera's cull mask and visibility layers), not by hiding things for everyone, so these rules can be added without a rewrite.

## Not now

Multiplayer is a later phase and out of scope. Spying and detection also raise questions about consent and what one family can see of another; those are for the founder when multiplayer is designed.
