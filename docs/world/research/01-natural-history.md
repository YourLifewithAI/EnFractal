# Research 1: the real animals and plants behind the traits

**For** [TRAITS.md](../TRAITS.md). Gathered 13 October 2026 with web search; no cost. Figures vary between sources, so ranges are given. These are grounding, not rules: the game borrows the character of each animal and leaves the rest.

## Red fox (*Vulpes vulpes*)

- **Size:** most adults weigh about 4.5 to 5 kg (range about 3.6 to 7.7 kg). The body is roughly 45 to 90 cm long, plus a long tail. [SUNY ESF](https://www.esf.edu/aec/adks/mammals/red_fox.htm); [Penn State Arboretum](https://arboretum.psu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Arboretum_Creature-Feature-Red-Fox_ACCESSIBLE.pdf)
- **Active:** mostly at night or twilight, more often by day when feeding young or on overcast days. [SUNY ESF](https://www.esf.edu/aec/adks/mammals/red_fox.htm); [Newfoundland and Labrador wildlife](https://www.gov.nl.ca/ffa/?p=4252)
- **Diet:** an omnivore and opportunist: carrion, fruit, berries and seeds, and animals such as mice, voles, rabbits and hares. [SUNY ESF](https://www.esf.edu/aec/adks/mammals/red_fox.htm)
- **Senses and hunting:** hearing is the main sense for finding prey in tall grass or under snow. A study of 592 hunting jumps by 84 wild foxes found that pounces aimed roughly north-east succeeded far more often (73% against about 18%). The authors suggest a magnetic sense; that is still a hypothesis. [Červený et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3097881)
- **Home:** a den in the ground, often taken over from other burrowing animals. Large systems have 7.6 to 23 m of tunnels, with mounds of soil at the entrances. Foxes cache surplus prey in the den or bury it. [SUNY ESF](https://www.esf.edu/aec/adks/mammals/red_fox.htm)
- **What the game borrows:** dusk and night activity, listening and pouncing, caching food (a find for the player), dens in banks, and curiosity together with wariness.

## Tawny owl (*Strix aluco*); barn owl (*Tyto alba*) as the alternative

- **Tawny owl size:** 37 to 46 cm long, wings 81 to 105 cm, about the size of a woodpigeon. Females are larger. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawny_owl); [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/birds/facts-about-tawny-owls)
- **Habits:** nocturnal and territorial, and does not migrate. It roosts by day in dense foliage or a tree trunk, and drops from a perch onto prey, which it swallows whole. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawny_owl)
- **Diet:** a generalist that prefers small rodents, but also takes birds, frogs, insects and sometimes fish. [Wildwood Trust](https://kent.wildwoodtrust.org/animals/tawny-owl)
- **Senses:** its ears sit asymmetrically, which gives excellent directional hearing; soft-fringed feathers make its flight silent. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawny_owl)
- **Nest:** usually a tree hole, often in an oak, or a nest box. Eggs come in February and March. [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/birds/facts-about-tawny-owls)
- **Defends its nest:** tawny owls defend their nest holes and may attack people. The photographer Eric Hosking lost an eye this way. [Museum für Naturkunde Berlin](https://naturblick.museumfuernaturkunde.berlin/species/portrait/46?lang=en); [GWCT](https://gwct.org.uk/wildlife/species-of-the-month/2015/december/)
- **Barn owl:** 33 to 39 cm long, wings about 85 to 93 cm, about 300 g. It hunts by sound over rough grassland, at dusk and dawn as well as at night. Field voles can be up to 85% of its diet. [Hawk and Owl Trust](https://hawkandowltrust.org/learn-and-discover/about-birds-of-prey/barn-owl); [Shropshire Wildlife Trust](https://shropshirewildlifetrust.org.uk/wildlife-explorer/birds/birds-prey/barn-owl)
- **What the game borrows:** the tawny owl's dusk call, roosting in dense trees and nesting in old trunks, hunting by ear (so a still player is safe), silent flight, and nest defence in spring.

## European rabbit (*Oryctolagus cuniculus*); eastern cottontail as the alternative

- **Size:** about 35 to 45 cm long and 1 to 2 kg; a buck can reach about 48 cm. [MSU Brain Museum](https://brains.anatomy.msu.edu/museum/brain/specimens/lagomorpha/domesticrabbit/index.html); [Better Planet Education](https://betterplaneteducation.org.uk/factsheets/rabbit-overview)
- **Warrens:** colonies in large burrows with many chambers and entrances. Rabbits rarely stray more than about 60 feet (18 m) from their burrow. [MSU Brain Museum](https://brains.anatomy.msu.edu/museum/brain/specimens/lagomorpha/domesticrabbit/index.html)
- **Active:** by day if undisturbed, but generally at night, dawn and dusk. [South Australian pest plan](https://cdn.environment.sa.gov.au/landscape/docs/ep/EPLB-pest-plan-European-Rabbit.pdf) (via a search summary; the PDF did not extract)
- **Alarm:** rabbits warn each other of danger by thumping their hind feet, and the white underside of the tail, seen as one runs, warns others nearby. [MSU Brain Museum](https://brains.anatomy.msu.edu/museum/brain/specimens/lagomorpha/domesticrabbit/index.html); [UK Nature](https://uknature.co.uk/mammals/O.cuniculus-info.html)
- **It shapes the land:** on chalk downland, rabbits keep the turf short, which holds back scrub and keeps the grassland open for wildflowers. When myxomatosis cut their numbers, coarse grass and scrub moved in. Conservation groups call rabbits ecosystem engineers. [Kent Wildlife Trust](https://kentwildlifetrust.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/KWT%20Land%20Mgt%20Advice_Sheet%203%20-%20Mgt%20of%20chalk%20grassland.pdf); [PTES](https://ptes.org/the-year-of-the-rabbit/)
- **Warrens house other animals:** camera traps at Australian warrens recorded 11 bird, 9 mammal and 8 reptile species at the entrances. [Australian Mammalogy](https://www.publish.csiro.au/am/am16032)
- **Eastern cottontail:** 0.9 to 1.8 kg. It does not dig warrens: it rests in a shallow scrape (a "form") and hides in brush piles and thickets. [UW–Stevens Point](https://www3.uwsp.edu/biology/VertebrateCollection/Pages/Vertebrates/Mammals%20of%20Wisconsin/Sylvilagus%20floridanus/Sylvilagus%20floridanus.aspx); [Missouri Dept. of Conservation](https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/eastern-cottontail-cottontail-rabbit)
- **What the game borrows:** the European rabbit, because warrens are homes the player can find. Also its grazed lawn as a visible effect on the land (and a firebreak), the thump and tail flash, and the dawn and dusk rhythm.

## Plants after fire

- **Heather** usually takes years to come back after a severe fire (an estimated four to five years at Headon Warren). Severe summer fires can destroy the seed bank. Heather regrows from seed, while most woody shrubs resprout from their roots. [Isle of Wight County Press](https://www.countypress.co.uk/news/26409916.isle-wight-headon-warren-wildfire-recovery-take-years/); [Natural England](https://publications.naturalengland.org.uk/file/61093); [Conservation Evidence](https://conservationevidencejournal.com/individual-study/6519)
- **Bracken (fern) and herbs** can come back more strongly after fire, helped by the nutrients ash releases. Wetter patches recover first. Rabbit grazing can slow the recovery of burnt ground. [Conservation Evidence](https://www.conservationevidence.com/individual-study/6509); [National Trust, Holt Heath](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/services/media/2026/august/holt-heath-fire-one-year-on)
- **What the game borrows,** with game time much faster than real time: grass and ferns come back first and heather slowly. Wet ground recovers sooner. Ash feeds new growth.

## Sizes at a glance (for decision 1)

The 10 cm player is treated as a 1.7 m person, so "true to the miniature" divides real sizes by about 17. "Storybook" divides by about 5.

| | Real | Miniature (÷17) | Storybook (÷5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rabbit, length | ~40 cm | ~2.4 cm | ~8 cm |
| Fox, shoulder height | ~35–40 cm | ~2.3 cm | ~8 cm |
| Fox, body plus tail | ~65 + 40 cm | ~4 + 2.5 cm | ~13 + 8 cm |
| Tawny owl, height | ~40 cm | ~2.4 cm | ~8 cm |
| Tawny owl, wingspan | ~95 cm | ~5.6 cm | ~19 cm |
| Pond fish (roach, perch) | ~15–25 cm | ~1–1.5 cm | ~3–5 cm (the game's fish are 1.5–4.5 cm) |

For comparison, the island's trees are about 0.3 to 0.9 m tall and its cottages about 0.4 m.
